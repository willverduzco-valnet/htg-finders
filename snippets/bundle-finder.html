Does bundling internet and cell service save you money?

We price every home internet + cell bundle available where you live and compare it with the cheapest way to buy the two separately, so you can see whether the discount is worth it.

How the bundle finder works

We price every home internet and cell bundle available where you live: a provider's home internet plus the cell plan it discounts, sized for your household's internet speed and your number of phone lines. Then we find the cheapest way to buy the two separately from providers and carriers with good coverage in your area, and compare the totals.

Home internet prices are first-year averages that include promos and equipment. Cell prices come from the carrier finder, which compares coverage where you live. When a company offers different discounts for different plans, we use the smallest one, so the savings we show are savings you can count on.

When a bundle makes sense

Bundles save the most when you already want both services from the same company, for example a family on Verizon or AT&T phone plans that can also get that company's fiber or 5G home internet. They save the least when the discount depends on an expensive phone plan: a cheaper carrier on the same network can cost less even without a discount. That's why the finder always shows the cheapest separate pair next to the bundles.

What to watch for

  • Discounts often require specific plans, autopay or paperless billing.
  • Promos and price locks end, so check the price after the first year. Read what a carrier's price lock really covers.
  • A bundle can make switching harder later, because canceling one service can raise the price of the other.
  • Some cell plans, such as Xfinity Mobile and Spectrum Mobile, are sold only to that company's home internet customers.

Frequently asked questions

Does bundling internet and cell service always save money?

No. In many areas, buying the two separately is cheaper, usually because a cheaper carrier on the same network costs less than the bundled phone plan. The finder tells you which way is cheaper where you live.

Why don't I see any bundles in my area?

Bundles depend on which providers serve your ZIP code and which of them discount a cell plan. If none does, the finder still shows the cheapest way to buy home internet and a cell plan separately.

How are "Featured" bundles chosen?

Featured bundles come from partner providers and appear first, labeled. The verdict at the top always compares the cheapest bundle with the cheapest separate pair.

Can I keep my phone number if I switch to a bundle?

Yes. You can move your number to a new carrier: get your account number and port-out PIN from your current carrier, and don't cancel your old plan until the new line works.
