Find the cell carrier that works where you do

Compare AT&T, T-Mobile, Verizon and dozens of cheaper carriers that run on their networks, using the coverage they report to the FCC for your home and the places you travel.

How the carrier finder works

AT&T, T-Mobile and Verizon each report their 4G LTE and 5G coverage to the FCC as detailed maps, which the FCC publishes in its Broadband Data Collection. The current data is the latest publicly available (checked November 11, 2026). It shows coverage as of December 31, 2025, because the FCC publishes each release several months after the date it describes. For each place you enter, we measure how much of it each network covers outdoors, at three levels: 4G LTE, 5G, and fast 5G (at least 35 Mbps down and 3 Mbps up).

Your home counts most, and the places you travel to share the rest. A place with poor coverage pulls the score down, because a dead zone at work matters even if home is fine. Then we pick each carrier's cheapest plan that fits how much data you use, how many lines you need and your must-haves, and rank the carriers on coverage and price.

Cheaper carriers use the same towers

Most carriers don't own a network. Mint Mobile, Visible, US Mobile, Cricket and dozens of others rent capacity on AT&T, T-Mobile or Verizon, so their coverage is the coverage of the network they use. The coverage table on the results page shows which network is strongest where you need it and which cheaper carriers run on it. The trade-offs: when a tower is busy, these carriers' data can be slowed before the network's own customers, some plans don't include the fastest 5G, and many carriers advertise prices that require paying for several months upfront. The must-haves let you filter for these.

What the must-haves mean

  • Hotspot: at least 10 GB of hotspot data each month to share with a laptop or tablet.
  • Priority data: data that isn't slowed behind the network's own customers when a tower is busy. On unlimited plans, we look for at least 50 GB of it.
  • Pay monthly: no prepaying 3, 6 or 12 months to get the advertised price.

If we can't confirm that a plan meets a must-have, we leave it out rather than guess.

How accurate is the coverage data?

The FCC maps show where carriers say they provide outdoor service. Signal indoors, in basements and in large buildings is often weaker, and real speeds change with how busy the network is. Frequently asked questions

Which carrier has the best coverage near me?

It depends on where you live and travel, because no network is the best everywhere. Enter your ZIP code and the places you go often: the coverage table shows each network's score at each place, and the map shows where its signal reaches.

Is a cheaper carrier's coverage worse than AT&T, T-Mobile or Verizon?

No. A cheaper carrier has the same coverage as the network it uses. The differences are priority when a tower is busy, access to the fastest 5G, roaming and customer service.

What is "fast 5G"?

Our name for 5G at 35 Mbps down and 3 Mbps up or faster in the FCC data. That is roughly where AT&T 5G+, T-Mobile 5G Ultra Capacity and Verizon 5G Ultra Wideband appear. The FCC records speed, not frequency band, so it's an approximation.

How current are the plans and prices?

We review plans and prices regularly, but carriers change their offers often, so confirm the price on the carrier's site before you sign up.

How does How-To Geek make money from this tool?

Some carriers pay us a commission when you sign up through our links, and partner carriers can appear first, labeled "Featured." Commissions never change coverage scores.
